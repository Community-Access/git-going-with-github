How do we keep them from piling up?

[ALEX]
How to Prevent Conflicts, Prevention is Easier Than Resolution: keep branches short-lived, sync with main frequently, communicate with your team, avoid mass reformatting, pull before you push, work in separate files when possible, keep pull requests small, and use draft PRs for early visibility.

[JAMIE]
That is a long list, but each item is practical.

[ALEX]
And each one lowers the chance that two people will hit the same lines at the same time.

[PAUSE]

[ALEX]
1. Keep your branches short-lived. The longer a branch sits, the more likely the rest of the project has moved on. Small, quick branches are easier to merge.

[ALEX]
2. Sync with main frequently. Pull or rebase often enough that your branch does not drift far from the current work.

[ALEX]
3. Communicate with your team. If you know which files or sections you are touching, say so early.

[ALEX]
4. Avoid mass reformatting. Big formatting-only changes can create avoidable conflicts for everyone else.

[ALEX]
5. Pull before you push. That keeps surprises smaller and pushes conflict detection closer to where the work happened.

[ALEX]
6. Work on separate files when possible. If different people own different files, they are less likely to collide.

[ALEX]
7. Keep PRs small. Smaller pull requests are easier to review and less likely to overlap in risky ways.

[ALEX]
8. Use Draft PRs for early visibility. A draft gives the team a chance to notice overlap before the branch is too far along.

[JAMIE]
That list turns prevention into habits instead of theory.

[ALEX]
That is why it works.

Conflict Markers - What They Mean: Git marks the file with three lines that frame the disagreement. The top section is your version, the middle divider separates the two sides, and the bottom section is their version.

[JAMIE]

[ALEX]
The common pattern is `<<<<<<< HEAD`, then the content from your version, then `=======`, then the incoming content, and finally `>>>>>>> branch-name`.

[PAUSE]

[ALEX]
Breakdown: `<<<<<<< HEAD` marks the start of the conflict, `=======` separates the two versions, and `>>>>>>> branch-name` marks the end and tells you which branch brought in the other change.

[JAMIE]
So the markers are a map, not just noise.

[ALEX]
Exactly. They show where the disagreement starts and ends.
